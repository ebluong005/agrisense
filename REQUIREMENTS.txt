AGRISENSE — PRODUCT & ENGINEERING REQUIREMENTS
Version: 2.0
Updated: 2026-11-24
Status: Working product specification
Suggested repository path: /REQUIREMENTS.txt

======================================================================
1. PRODUCT VISION
======================================================================
Mission: Make useful crop and drought monitoring accessible to small and
resource-constrained farmers without requiring expensive on-farm hardware,
paid satellite imagery, or specialist data-analysis skills.

Positioning: AgriSense is an affordable, mobile-friendly field-monitoring
and early-warning tool. It combines publicly accessible weather and
satellite observations into clear, explainable signals that help farmers
prioritize which fields to inspect.

Product promise: A farmer can register a field, understand available
weather and vegetation trends, and see possible issues in plain language.
AgriSense DOES NOT claim to measure on-farm soil moisture directly,
diagnose crop diseases, guarantee yields, or determine exact irrigation
amounts without validated local measurements and agronomic models.

Initial market: Small and resource-constrained farms in the United
States, with an initial pilot focused on one geographic area and a limited
set of crops. Expansion to other countries requires separate evaluation
of language, connectivity, data coverage, and agronomic suitability.

Business hypothesis (not yet validated): Farmers may adopt a free basic
service, while some will pay for advanced history/alerts; cooperatives,
nonprofits, or extension organizations may fund access for farmers.

======================================================================
2. USERS AND CORE PROBLEMS
======================================================================
Primary user: Farmer or farm operator with limited time, budget, and
technical expertise, often using a smartphone rather than a desktop.

Secondary users (future): Agricultural cooperatives, extension agents,
nonprofits, and farm advisers assisting multiple growers.

Problems to solve:
- Existing crop-monitoring workflows may rely on field visits, scattered
  weather apps, and spreadsheets.
- Some precision-agriculture solutions require costly hardware, ongoing
  subscriptions, or specialized expertise.
- Vegetation indices and drought data can be difficult to interpret.
- Limited connectivity and small/irregular fields can restrict the value
  of some geospatial products.

Core jobs-to-be-done:
- "Help me check which field needs attention today."
- "Show me whether recent rainfall is unusually low."
- "Explain whether vegetation has changed, and how reliable that is."
- "Let me use this without buying sensors or learning satellite science."

======================================================================
3. PRODUCT PRINCIPLES
======================================================================
P1. Affordable by design: no dedicated sensor or drone required for core
    functionality; control cloud processing and bandwidth costs.
P2. Simple by default: actionable plain-language summaries before charts.
P3. Mobile-first: readable on a phone; usable outdoors and on slow links.
P4. Evidence-based: show source, observation date, units, and uncertainty.
P5. Transparent limitations: distinguish precipitation estimates, regional
    drought classifications, vegetation signals, and actual soil conditions.
P6. Privacy: farmer controls access to their farms and field locations.
P7. Progressive complexity: advanced maps and history remain optional.

======================================================================
4. RELEASE SCOPE AND PRIORITIES
======================================================================
P0 = Required for first publicly testable MVP.
P1 = Next iteration after real user feedback.
P2 = Future experiments; not needed for initial launch.

P0 — REQUIRED
R-001 Accounts and secure farm ownership
  - Sign up, log in, log out; hash passwords securely.
  - Every protected farm/field endpoint enforces owner authorization.
  - Users can export or delete their own farm records.

R-002 Simple farm and field onboarding
  - Create/edit/delete a farm with a name and representative location.
  - Add one or more fields by map polygon; provide a simpler point-based
    fallback if drawing is too difficult on a phone.
  - Persist field geometry using PostgreSQL/PostGIS or validated GeoJSON
    with a documented migration path to PostGIS.
  - Show hectares/acres and clarify that area is an estimate.
  - Provide guidance when a field is too small or irregular for reliable
    satellite interpretation.

R-003 Historical weather monitoring
  - Fetch actual historical precipitation and temperature from NASA POWER
    or another documented provider with appropriate usage terms.
  - Show 7-day and 30-day rainfall totals and historical temperature trend.
  - Show weather-data spatial granularity and source date; NEVER present
    gridded weather data as a physical sensor reading from the field.
  - Indicate missing observations and temporary provider failures.

R-004 Field vegetation monitoring
  - Show latest valid Sentinel-2 NDVI observation and historical trend
    where coverage, cloud conditions, field size, and provider access permit.
  - Apply cloud/cloud-shadow masking and expose the valid-pixel percentage
    or comparable quality indicator.
  - Display observation date and a clear "no usable image" state.
  - Compare observations with relevant recent/historical context; do not
    automatically infer crop disease or drought cause from NDVI alone.
  - If Copernicus credentials/quotas are unavailable, the interface must
    transparently show vegetation monitoring as unavailable, not fake data.

R-005 Plain-language dashboard
  - Mobile-responsive home screen lists farms/fields and last update time.
  - Give a simple status for each field: No clear issue / Check field /
    Insufficient data, backed by transparent rules.
  - Provide an explanation linking status to observations and thresholds.
  - Offer charts and a map as secondary detail, not the only interface.
  - Include loading, empty, stale-data, and provider-error states.

R-006 Explainable early warnings
  - Rule-based flags for sustained low rainfall and significant NDVI
    decline, when there are sufficient valid observations.
  - Each warning records field, rule, data source, observation dates,
    and a suggested low-risk follow-up (e.g., inspect the field).
  - Never prescribe exact irrigation amounts or give definitive disease
    diagnoses from these signals.
  - Prevent duplicate alerts for the same trigger/time period.

R-007 Accessibility and constrained-connectivity design
  - Core flows work on a small mobile viewport and with keyboard input.
  - Basic field summary is visible without loading heavyweight map tiles.
  - Compress assets, cache appropriate read-only data, and avoid mandatory
    continuous satellite imagery downloads.
  - Make source dates visible even when cached data is displayed.

R-008 Reliability and transparency
  - Document all third-party data dependencies and their terms.
  - Validate latitude/longitude, dates, polygons, and request payloads.
  - Add provider timeouts, safe retries, cache TTLs, and structured logs.
  - Never silently substitute mock observations into production responses.

P1 — NEXT ITERATION
R-101 U.S. Drought Monitor regional D0–D4 classifications with weekly

R-102 Optional email/SMS notifications with opt-in and frequency controls.
R-103 Crop type and planting-date context for interpreting seasonal NDVI.
R-104 Low-bandwidth mode, improved offline-friendly cached summaries, and
      multilingual onboarding based on pilot needs.
R-105 Field notes and scouting photos recorded by farmers.
R-106 CSV data export and printable concise field report.
R-107 Basic cohort onboarding and usage analytics without excessive

P2 — FUTURE / NOT IN MVP
R-201 Validated crop-specific agronomic models and irrigation advice.
R-202 Sensor integrations where farmers already own compatible equipment.
R-203 Multi-farm dashboards for cooperatives/extension organizations.
R-204 Yield prediction, disease diagnosis, and machine learning only after

R-205 International expansion and alternative satellite data sources.
R-206 Paid subscriptions and organization-sponsored access after demand

5. KEY USER FLOWS
======================================================================
FLOW A — FIRST-TIME SETUP
1. User creates account and signs in.
2. User creates a farm by address, coordinates, or map selection.
3. User draws a field boundary OR starts with a representative point.
4. System validates and stores the field.
5. Dashboard displays available weather, NDVI, and freshness/quality info.
6. If data is missing, a clear reason and fallback are shown.

FLOW B — DAILY FIELD CHECK
1. User opens AgriSense on a phone.
2. User sees each field's status and last valid observation.
3. User selects a flagged field.
4. System explains the signal, supporting measurements, and limitations.
5. User decides whether to inspect the field; may record a note in P1.

FLOW C — SERVICE FAILURE
1. Weather or satellite provider is slow, rate-limited, or unavailable.
2. App serves recently cached observations when safe and labels them stale.
3. App shows a non-technical error and never invents fresh measurements.
4. A retry/backoff policy avoids excessive provider calls.

6. TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS AND ARCHITECTURE
======================================================================
Frontend: React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS, charting library; use
MapLibre or Leaflet for optional interactive maps.
Backend: Python, FastAPI, Pydantic, SQLAlchemy, HTTPX, Alembic.
Database: PostgreSQL with PostGIS for farm/field geometry.
Architecture: modular monolith; separate routes, schemas, service
integrations, business rules, and persistence.

Recommended deployment target:
- Frontend: Vercel static/Vite deployment.
- Backend: Render, Railway, or other Python-capable host; deploy separately
  from the frontend, with background tasks/worker if required.
- Database: managed PostgreSQL with PostGIS enabled (e.g., a supported
  Supabase configuration or another managed PostgreSQL provider).
- Local development: Docker Compose for PostgreSQL/PostGIS; frontend and
  FastAPI may run locally for rapid debugging, or all services via Compose.
- On Apple Silicon, prefer native linux/arm64 images, not x86 emulation.

API requirements:
- Versioned /api/v1 endpoints with OpenAPI/Swagger docs.
- Consistent JSON structures and appropriate HTTP status codes.
- Pagination for lists as data volume grows.
- Server-side authorization on every user-owned resource.

Data model (minimum):
- users: id, auth fields, created_at
- farms: id, owner_id, name, coordinates, created_at
- fields: id, farm_id, name, geometry, area, created_at
- weather_observations: location/time/source, metrics, fetched_at
- vegetation_observations: field/time/source, NDVI, valid_pixel_fraction
- alerts: field_id, rule_id, observation window, state, created_at
Later: crop_seasons, drought_observations, subscriptions, organizations.

Operational requirements:
- Migration files checked into source control; no destructive migration
  without an explicit review and data migration plan.
- Secrets only in environment variables or a secret manager.
- TLS in production and restricted CORS for production frontend origin.
- Password hashes, strong session/token management, rate limiting on auth.
- Database backups, error logging, and health endpoints.
- Unit tests for rules, API integration tests, and basic frontend tests.
- Do not log precise farm coordinates unnecessarily.

======================================================================
7. NONFUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
======================================================================
NFR-001 Affordability: Track per-active-farm satellite/API processing,
        storage, hosting, and notification costs before setting prices.
NFR-002 Speed target: cached basic dashboard summary should typically
        load within 3 seconds on a reasonable mobile connection; measure.
NFR-003 Availability: external API outage must not block access to saved
        farms or previously cached observations.
NFR-004 Data integrity: store observation date, fetch time, units, source,
        and missing-data markers for every derived metric.
NFR-005 Accessibility: accessible labels, contrast, keyboard navigation,
        touch-friendly controls, and readable status without color alone.
NFR-006 Security: owner isolation, safe credentials, and protected exports.
NFR-007 Privacy: clear farm-location data policy and deletion mechanism.
NFR-008 Portability: document local setup, deployment steps, and rollback.

======================================================================
8. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA — P0 MVP
======================================================================
AC-01 A new user can sign up, sign in, and create a farm from a phone.
AC-02 A user can save a field and see it after refreshing/restarting app.
AC-03 An unauthorized user cannot view, edit, or delete another user's
      farms or fields; tests cover this boundary.
AC-04 The system retrieves real historical temperature and precipitation
      and displays correct units, period, and data source.
AC-05 For suitable fields and valid satellite observations, the app shows
      dated NDVI statistics and a trend; otherwise it clearly explains
      unavailability or low confidence.
AC-06 Every "check field" signal provides a readable rule explanation,
      supporting dates/values, and a non-diagnostic caution.
AC-07 The main dashboard works on a typical phone-sized viewport without
      requiring a desktop or advanced map interaction.
AC-08 Service outages do not wipe saved data or result in invented values.
AC-09 Local installation instructions work on an ARM64 macOS machine.
AC-10 Automated tests pass and README documents known limitations.
AC-11 The frontend is deployable on Vercel, API on a Python host, and data
      persists in managed PostgreSQL/PostGIS.

======================================================================
9. SUCCESS METRICS AND VALIDATION PLAN
======================================================================
Do not confuse registered accounts with proof of farmer value.
Initial validation: interview 10–15 farmers in one target segment; run a
small pilot with 5–10 farms, subject to informed consent.

Primary learning metric:
- Percentage of pilot users who report a useful field-inspection or farm
  management decision informed by AgriSense in a given month.

Supporting metrics:
- Successful farm/field onboarding rate.
- Time to first useful field summary.
- Weekly returning pilot users.
- Percentage of fields with a recent valid satellite observation.
- Alert accuracy/false-positive feedback from farmer follow-up.
- Cost per active farm per month and API quota utilization.

Pricing hypothesis to test, NOT a committed price:
- Free basic tier for one or a limited number of fields.
- Optional roughly $5–10/month enhanced tier if users demonstrate value.
- Potential organization-funded access for cooperatives and nonprofits.

10. DEVELOPMENT ORDER
======================================================================
Phase 1: Stabilize existing FastAPI/NASA POWER service; Docker ARM64
         PostgreSQL/PostGIS; SQLAlchemy models, migrations, auth, farms.
Phase 2: Mobile-first React onboarding, field management, weather summary.
Phase 3: Sentinel-2 NDVI integration with quality/freshness indicators.
Phase 4: Explainable rule-based alerts and resilient caching.
Phase 5: Security/testing/accessibility review, README, Vercel + API +
         managed database deployment, small farmer pilot.
Phase 6: Prioritize P1 features from observed pilot needs, not assumptions.

======================================================================
11. EXPLICIT NON-GOALS AND RISKS
======================================================================
Non-goals for P0:
- No claims of real-time field soil-moisture measurement.
- No definitive disease identification based on NDVI.
- No automated precise irrigation prescriptions.
- No custom drone hardware, paid sensor requirement, or complicated ML.
- No unsupported claims of guaranteed crop yields or water savings.

Key risks and mitigations:
- Small/mixed fields: warn about sparse/mixed pixels and lower confidence.
- Clouds/revisit gaps: display last valid date; don't imply live status.
- Regional weather resolution: disclose difference from local sensors.
- Financial sustainability: measure unit economics before final pricing.
- Competition from free satellite apps: differentiate through simplicity,
  resource-constrained workflows, contextual alerts, and farmer testing.
- Connectivity: keep core dashboard lightweight and cache safely.
- Data governance: minimize location sharing and protect ownership.

======================================================================
12. DEFINITION OF DONE
======================================================================
A small farmer can sign up, add a field, view dated real weather and
available vegetation observations, understand clear field-inspection
signals, and use the service on a phone without buying dedicated hardware.
The system is tested, documented, respects user data boundaries, handles
missing environmental data honestly, and can be deployed affordably.

End of requirements.
